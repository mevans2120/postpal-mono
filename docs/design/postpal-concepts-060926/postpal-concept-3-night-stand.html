Concept 3 · The Night Stand

Lens: Swiss Functional, Night-First · Schibsted Grotesk / Spline Sans Mono · THE task-led concept
This is the one concept that leads with the task list — and the list is meds only. The research's single executive job (the AVC days 1–5 grid, 6–8 dose events/day, done while opioid-foggy) is the entire home screen. Hydration, walking, and monitoring never appear as tasks anywhere. → Principle 2 taken literally: the med schedule is the only checklist.
Designed at 2:14 AM, then adapted to daytime — not the reverse. Dark evergreen surfaces, candle-amber accents, 40px mono countdown readable at arm's length with one eye open. Night mode is the default state during days 0–5; the white "day mode" is the variant. → Principle 4: if it doesn't work on opioids at 2 AM, it doesn't work. The 2 AM fever-Google moment is when stakes peak and the clinic line is closed.
One decision at a time, with guardrails doing the math. The hero is the next dose, full stop. "Pain is bad now" resolves PRN ambiguity (what can I take, how much is left) and the Tylenol meter tracks the 4000 mg ceiling so a foggy patient never does arithmetic. → AVC grid specifics: scheduled ibuprofen/cyclobenzaprine vs PRN hydromorphone/Zofran is a genuinely confusing structure; the instrument absorbs it.
The quiet card is the emotional core. "Nothing else is asked of you until 3:00... the schedule is watching the clock, not you." Permission to rest is the reassurance this concept offers, plus tonight's expected-symptom line (waves of cramping) so the dark hours don't read as emergencies. → Care-tracker's strongest prior insight (quiet-hours messaging) + Principle 1 woven into a task-led frame.
The support person is a first-class operator. "Sister's phone paired" — Tasha sees the same schedule and can log doses. In the research, the support person administers the meds for the first 72 hours; here she's in the header, not a settings toggle. → JTBD 6: "let my person help me." Scoped to the acute phase, then the pairing expires.
Escalation splits into check vs call. "Is this normal?" (symptom checker with AVC thresholds) sits beside "Call the clinic line" at the bottom of every screen — interpret first, escalate in one tap, 911 signs inside the call sheet. → Principle 7: thresholds verbatim from the packet (≥101°F, pad-per-hour, foul smell).
The instrument retires itself. When the med grid ends (~day 5–7), the home screen stops being a schedule: day mode lightens, and by week 2 the product becomes the cycle scoreboard with an MRI countdown. No zombie checklist lingering past its usefulness. → Principle 6 + anti-reference: not a productivity app. The task UI exists exactly as long as the executive job does.
