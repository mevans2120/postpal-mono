PostPal · Design Concepts · June 2026

Mood Board: A good nurse's house call

Three directions for a clinic-prescribed UAE/UFE recovery companion. Every choice below is graded against one scene: a nurse who comes to your home, checks what matters, answers the scary question plainly, and leaves you calmer than she found you.

Design intent

How she should feel

reassured believed steady

What this must never feel like

a hospital portal (MyChart) a cutesy period app a productivity app

Anti-references do real work here. No compliance gray and form-stacks (portal). No petals, pinks, or euphemism (period app). No streaks, scores, or progress-guilt (productivity) — the research's third principle bans grading recovery against fixed days.

The shared motif: the recovery line

Tia's identity uses a thin winding line for varied health journeys. PostPal borrows the idea with a stricter meaning: every recovery is a different curve through the same wide, normal band. The band-and-curve appears in all three concepts — it is the visual argument for "benchmark against the range, never grade against the day."

Three directions

LENS: CONVERSATIONAL · ORGANIC
1 · The Nurse Line

Leads with the question, not the schedule. Home screen asks "Is something worrying you?" The interpreter is the product.

Type — Petrona + Albert Sans

A low fever today is expected.
Your body is responding to the embolization — most women run warm through day 3. Call us if it reaches 101°F.

Imagery & illustration

No photography in-app. Hand-traced botanical line work (single-weight, clay ink) as quiet punctuation — closer to a naturalist's notebook than a spa. Photos in marketing get a warm paper-grain overlay, never glossy.

LENS: EDITORIAL · STORY-DRIVEN
2 · The Recovery Journal

Each day is a page. Honest, range-based prose with margin notes from the clinic — a friend's annotated journal, professionally fact-checked.

Type — Newsreader + Hanken Grotesk

Day 5 of about fourteen
The fog usually starts lifting around now — though plenty of recoveries take until week two. Both are normal.

Imagery & illustration

Editorial restraint: no photos in the daily pages. Diagrammatic ink illustrations (annotated curves, anatomical sketches with handwriting-style labels) in oxblood and graphite. Hairline rules and a ragged-right column do the aesthetic work.

LENS: SWISS FUNCTIONAL · NIGHT-FIRST
3 · The Night Stand

The one task-led concept — and the task list is meds only. Designed for 2 AM on opioids: dark, low-glare, one decision at a time.

Imagery & illustration

None. The instrument aesthetic is typographic: mono timestamps, hairline progress arcs, generous dark space. The only "image" is the amber countdown arc, dimmed to candle-light luminance after 10 PM.

Why these choices (against the research)

ChoiceResearch insight it serves
Three different home screens (question / day-page / next dose) and only one is a listTask-timeline verdict: patients compare, they don't complete; the med grid is the only true checklist
The band-and-curve motif everywherePrinciple 3: benchmark against the range, never grade against the day — fixed-day milestones read as failure to slow recoverers
