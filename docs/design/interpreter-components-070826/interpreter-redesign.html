Answer first, no call button — one design, three states

The old interpreter ended with "Call Carrie" as the loud primary button, training a call even after the worry was resolved. Here the closing question is the action: "Yes, that helps" resolves and logs it; "Not quite" surfaces the line to watch and what to try now. There's no clinic-contact button at all: the clinic isn't always open, and the right first step for the "app couldn't answer" case is a separate problem to design next. Only a 911 line stays, for a true emergency. Example: the day-1 "cramping is intense" worry.

How the three approaches show up

1 · Resolution is the primary action. The loud clay button is now "Yes, that helps", not "Call Carrie". The most prominent thing on the sheet confirms the reassurance instead of inviting a call.

2 · "Did this answer your question?" The ask and the action are the same control. "Yes" closes and records the worry on today's page — a call the app absorbed, and a number the clinic can see. "Not quite" is the only route that opens a path to a person, so contact happens exactly when the information failed.

3 · The clinic's line stays information. The threshold criterion appears as the one thing to watch for, never as a contact button. Per your call, there's no "reach the clinic" action anywhere: the clinic has closed hours, and the right first step for the "app couldn't answer" case is a separate problem to design next.

What's left open (on purpose)

Only the 911 line remains, for a true emergency. With the clinic-contact button gone, the "app couldn't answer" case ends in the watch-line, self-care, and a check-back, with no routine path to a person. That's deliberate for now: the clinic isn't always open, and a better first step is still to be designed. Open tension to resolve then: design principle #4 (a route to a human) and the liability line will both need an answer.
